Framework

A controlled method for reconciling grid-asset evidence

OGARD combines explicit identity regimes, preserved evidence, versioned reconciliation, authorized review, contradiction handling, and operator-governed outputs.

Defined problem

Integration is not only a joining problem

The same physical equipment may be represented by a functional location in a maintenance system, a serial-numbered unit in an asset register, and one or more tags in a historian. Each representation may be valid for its own purpose while remaining incomplete for cross-system reasoning.

OGARD does not create a new universal system of record. It creates an auditable interpretation layer while preserving the source evidence that supports or challenges that interpretation.

A governed asset view must not erase the records that support or challenge it.

Equality in one identity regime must not imply equality across the others. Replacement, relocation, tag drift, and late-arriving records must remain explainable through time and evidence.

Five components

The framework at a glance

01

Canonical asset and evidence

Separates governed asset context from source records and preserves provenance, time, and review state.

02

Layered architecture

Retains raw evidence, makes conformance explicit, and carries lineage into controlled outputs.

03

Tiered reconciliation

Uses deterministic rules first, reproducible scoring second, and review where evidence remains insufficient.

04

Contradiction and status

Preserves disagreement and permits only operator-governed status derivation with explicit safeguards.

05

Governance and controls

Defines authority, security, observability, change control, auditability, and production-readiness boundaries.

Reconciliation workflow

Deterministic where possible, reviewed where necessary

  1. 1

    Bound candidates

    Use location, asset class, identifiers, approved aliases, and time windows to create a controlled candidate set.

  2. 2

    Apply versioned rules

    Accept explicit relationships only through documented, tested, and effective-dated deterministic logic.

  3. 3

    Score incomplete evidence

    Use reproducible similarity methods only for approved sources and asset classes, with declared thresholds.

  4. 4

    Review and record

    Route ambiguous, contradictory, or consequential cases to authorized review and retain the full decision record.

Reference status vocabulary

Operator-governed status with a safe default

StatusReference meaningMinimum interpretation safeguard
AvailableApproved evidence supports expected availability within the defined scope.Does not prove absence of latent defect. Coverage, freshness, and contradictions remain operator-defined.
DegradedApproved evidence supports impaired condition or reduced capability under an operator-defined rule.Requires asset-class-specific criteria, time validity, and authorized engineering interpretation.
OfflineApproved evidence supports that the asset is unavailable, disconnected, or out of service.Must distinguish planned, forced, temporary, and data-quality conditions.
At riskApproved evidence indicates a condition requiring attention without supporting Degraded or Offline.Must not be presented as failure prediction. Indicators, expiry, and review requirements must be defined.
UnknownEvidence is insufficient, stale, ambiguous, outside scope, or materially contradictory.Unknown is the safe default whenever another status cannot be justified.
Contribution boundary

Complementary to existing standards and tools

Master data management

MDM can support golden records, lineage, and history. OGARD adds grid-specific identity regimes, time-valid installation context, contradiction as an explicit output, and operator authority.

IEC Common Information Model

CIM provides shared utility semantics. OGARD offers an evidence reconciliation and governance workflow and includes only an indicative, non-normative concept crosswalk.

Entity-resolution tooling

General tools can support blocking, scoring, and matching. OGARD defines the utility identity semantics, review boundaries, contradiction controls, and traceability expected around those techniques.

In scope

What OGARD directly addresses

  • Electricity-network asset evidence
  • Cross-system identity reconciliation
  • Preserved provenance and contradiction
  • Human review and audit records
  • Operator-governed derived status
Not claimed

What v0.1 does not establish

  • Live utility performance or production suitability
  • Operator adoption or outage reduction
  • Autonomous diagnosis or failure prediction
  • Formal CIM compliance or certification
  • Executed or independently reproduced benchmark results
